How It Works

A temporary email address is a real, working email address that exists for a short time only — here, 60 minutes. You use it the same way you would use any other email address: give it to a website, receive the confirmation code or activation link it sends, and then walk away. When the hour is over, the address stops working and its mail disappears with it.

That short lifetime is exactly what makes it useful. Your real inbox is valuable — it holds your bank alerts, your password resets, and years of personal mail — yet every newsletter, shop, forum, and free trial asks for it. Each sign-up is another chance for that address to be sold, leaked, or buried under marketing mail for years. A temporary address takes the risk instead: it absorbs the one message you actually need, and the spam that would have followed it has nowhere to go once the address is gone.

  1. Copy your temporary addressThe address at the top of this page is already yours — no sign-up and no password. Choose the Yandex or Gmail tab, then tap "Copy Email". Tap "New Email" any time you want a different address.
  2. Use it wherever an email is asked forPaste the address into the sign-up form, download page, or trial you are dealing with, and submit it like a normal email address.
  3. Read the mail hereThe inbox on this page checks for new mail automatically every few seconds, and there is a Refresh button if you are in a hurry. Verification codes are highlighted with their own Copy button, so you can grab a code without even opening the message.
  4. Finish before the timer runs outThe countdown on the address card shows how much of the 60 minutes is left. If you close the page, keep your address code — you can reopen the same inbox with it from the "Reopen your previous inbox" box, as long as the hour has not run out.

One honest warning: a temporary address is for quick, one-time jobs. Never use it for banking, government services, or any account you would be upset to lose, because once the address expires there is no way to receive password resets or account notices on it again.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does my temporary email address last?

Every address works for 60 minutes from the moment it is created. The countdown on the address card always shows the time remaining, and when it reaches zero the address expires and its mail is no longer shown.

Is MR ZACKY Temp Mail free?

Yes, completely free. There is no sign-up, no account, and no payment — open the page, copy the address, and use it.

Can I send or reply to emails from my temporary address?

No. This service is receive-only: it is built for receiving confirmation codes, activation links, and one-time messages. If you need a conversation, use your regular email account for that.

Is it safe to use a temporary email address?

For sign-ups, trials, and verification codes, yes — it keeps your real address out of the hands of sites you do not fully trust. But treat your address code like a key: anyone who knows the code can open the same inbox, so never use a temporary address for sensitive information such as banking or personal documents.

What happens after the 60 minutes are over?

The address stops working and its messages are no longer displayed. Nothing is kept for later — just tap "New Email" to get a fresh address with a new 60-minute window.

Can I restore an old inbox?

Yes, within its lifetime. If you closed the page, scroll to "Reopen your previous inbox", enter the code from your address, and the same inbox reopens — as long as its 60 minutes have not run out. After expiry, an inbox cannot be restored.

Do you store my data?

We do not ask for your name, phone number, or any personal details, and there are no accounts to store data in. The only thing saved is your current address code, in your own browser, so your inbox can reopen if you close the page. Messages are kept only so they can be shown to you during the address's short lifetime. Our Privacy Policy explains this in full.

Which email providers are supported?

You can choose between two providers using the tabs at the top of the page: Yandex (@yandex.com) and Gmail (@gmail.com). Both work the same way, with the same 60-minute lifetime — if one address is refused by a website, try the other.
